# Road-test battery analysis — 2026-09-11

Analyzed the preserved daily log (local-only evidence)
from the Sep 10 21:12 battery candidate (`19ad9ee`). Raw calculations are in
battery-analysis.json (local-only evidence),
regenerated by `python3 tools/ride_battery_analysis.py`.

## Battery result

**About 12 hours of similar use is the best short-window estimate.** This is
not a full-discharge result or proof of an improvement over the previous build.
The battery gauge reported 1500 mAh full capacity and 100% health.

| Observed configuration | Window | Capacity used | Average from capacity change | Nominal full-battery projection |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Phone + Favero Assioma power meter, with stop/display/hunt activity | 08:36:22–08:46:17 | 21 mAh / 595 s | 127 mA | 11.8 h |
| Assioma connected, phone absent, resumed ride | 09:37:23–09:44:49 | 16 mAh / 446 s | 129 mA | 11.6 h |
| Stopped, phone and meter disconnected | 08:53:43–08:58:40 | 8 mAh / 297 s | 97 mA | 15.5 h |

Formula: average mA = capacity drop × 3600 / elapsed seconds; runtime = 1500 /
average mA. Each comparison stays within one boot and precedes USB charging.
The riding-window endpoints have 1 mAh resolution, giving approximately
11–12.4 h from quantization alone; gauge calibration, temperature, load changes,
and the short sample duration add uncertainty beyond that range.

Sparse instantaneous readings average 161 mA in the first window and 114 mA
in the resumed window. Those snapshots sample different parts of the display
and sensor-search cycle; they should not be treated as a continuous average.
Capacity change is more useful for estimating aggregate drain, though the
fuel gauge can adjust its remaining-capacity estimate as conditions change.

The first reading was 1498 mAh / 100%; the last reading before ride save was
1372 mAh / 92% at 09:44:49. The 126 mAh drop spans about 71 wall-clock minutes,
including stops, crashes, recovery screens and an idle deep shutdown. Dividing
that whole period by the drop would overstate riding endurance. The saved FIT
summary reports only 1101 active seconds plus 80 paused seconds; neither is a
valid denominator for the whole-outing battery drop.

## What the ride validates

- **CPU sleep occurred:** 42,227 successful calls and 34 rejected calls in 45
  logged windows totaling 2722 seconds. Time inside sleep calls totaled
  818.22 seconds, or 30.1% of those windows. This includes entry/exit overhead;
  windows include pre-ride/stopped/recovery time and omit buffered data lost
  before resets. It is not the fraction of all moving time spent asleep.
- **GPS receive corruption was largely resolved:** 170 windows with actual
  sleep contained 940,628 NMEA bytes, 16,161 checksum-valid sentences, and zero
  corrupt or truncated sentences. One missing GGA/RMC epoch was recorded at
  08:42:22; this was not a perfectly lossless result. Another startup gap at
  09:07:37 occurred before any sleep and is excluded from that sleep total.
- **SD remained usable:** all 45 PM windows report mounted SD, both watchdog
  reboots mounted on the first try, and the resumed ride was saved at 5.79 km.
  No dedicated read-back SD stress test ran during this ride, so a successful
  recording is the evidence here, not a full filesystem integrity test.
- **Real sensor notifications continued through sleep:** the Assioma operated
  at 30 ms during the first outing and 50 ms after the second reboot. The first
  connection delivered 1037 notifications with a 1080 ms maximum gap before
  ending at 08:51:53 (reason 531 / remote termination). This is not the
  supervision-timeout reason and does not establish a sleep-induced failure.

## What has not passed

**Overall stability did not pass:** two interrupt-watchdog resets. See the
[crash investigation](ride-crashes-2026-09-11.md); neither retained a stack dump.

**Phone continuity did not pass:** 13 disconnects with reason 520 (supervision
timeout) between 08:47:32 and 08:49:09. The phone-sleep fallback disarmed after
three. Similar phone timeouts occurred after USB was reconnected and CPU sleep
was held off, so the logs do not establish sleep as their cause. Physical
separation or phone-side behavior around the stop remains unknown. Some
reconnections used a short 720 ms supervision timeout; that deserves separate
investigation if the phone remained beside the bike.

**GPS acquisition remains slow:** the first logged own-receiver fix took 509
seconds; the recorder used phone position as fallback earlier. Warm reboots
had an immediate receiver fix. The separate AGNSS implementation was not on
this ride's firmware.

There was no matched awake/sleep A/B road test or complete battery discharge.
The road data supports working sleep and lower current in quieter states, but
cannot quantify the percentage improvement caused by the changes. The older
14.9–16.6 h bench projections should not be advertised as validated road
endurance. Crash fixes and phone-link investigation remain necessary before
calling this candidate reliable.

## Follow-up firmware

After retrieving the ride evidence, crash reports with NVS staging and verified
SD delivery were implemented and tested on the board. An unrelated bench panic
captured the UART event task's logging path; UART warnings now use the deferred
driver logger and that task has a 4 KB stack in the battery profile. A subsequent
UART warning and deliberate crash/recovery test completed successfully. See
[crash-report implementation and validation](../docs/crash-reports.md).
These changes were not on the ride analyzed above, and do not establish that
the two field interrupt-watchdog resets are fixed or change its battery estimate.

Raw logs, crash dumps, and exported investigation results are retained locally in ignored paths. They must not be committed: diagnostic breadcrumbs and captured memory can contain GPS coordinates.
